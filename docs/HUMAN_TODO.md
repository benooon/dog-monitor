## Legal (from the weekly legal review, 2026-11-11; I am not a lawyer)

- [ ] **Operator details.** Decide who operates the app (you personally or a company) and fill the `[...]` fields in `docs/legal/*`: legal name, address, privacy email, support email, court city.
- [ ] **Lawyer sign-off before launch.** Have an Israeli privacy lawyer review `docs/legal/*` and `docs/PRIVACY.md`, especially: recording of third parties and sound, consent for the training opt-in, limits of liability, and whether the Privacy Protection Law (Amendment 13) database duties apply (registration, a privacy officer or database-security documentation, depending on size).
- [ ] **Sign data processing agreements** (DPAs) with Railway, LiveKit Cloud and your bucket provider (Cloudflare or Railway), and keep them. Most offer a click-through DPA in their dashboard.
- [ ] **Pick and write down the data regions** (Railway project region, bucket region, LiveKit project region). The policy must say them. EU and Israel users: prefer EU regions.
- [ ] **EU/UK representative**: only if you want to market to EU/UK users from outside the EU (GDPR Art. 27). Ask the lawyer.
- [ ] **Host the policy.** Publish the privacy policy and terms at a public URL (the backend `/privacy` task will serve them) and put the URL in App Store Connect.
- [ ] **Apple Sign in revocation key.** Create a Sign in with Apple key (.p8) in the developer account so the backend can revoke the Apple token on account deletion (Apple requirement). Add it as env vars when the backlog task is ready.
- [ ] **Breach process.** Agree who gets alerted and who reports (Israel: Privacy Protection Authority for serious incidents; EU: 72 hours). One paragraph in `docs/PRIVACY.md` once decided.

- [ ] **Waitlist admin token (Railway):** set `ADMIN_TOKEN` (`openssl rand -hex 32`) on the backend service to read sign-up counts: `curl -H "Authorization: Bearer $ADMIN_TOKEN" https://<railway host>/waitlist/stats`. Without it the stats route stays hidden; sign-ups are still stored.
